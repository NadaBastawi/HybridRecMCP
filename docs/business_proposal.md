# AI-Powered Customer Service Recommendation System — Independent Proof of Concept

**Prepared by Nada Bastawi · HybridRecMCP · Independent portfolio project**

## The opportunity

A service team may need to review property details, prior visits and potential preventive
follow-ups before recommending its next conversation with a customer. A consistent
review assistant could help staff organize that information and focus their questions.
This proposal does not assume that a particular company lacks such capabilities.

## What the prototype demonstrates

A local interactive screen accepts a new property profile or an existing synthetic
customer. It presents relevant service ideas, a separate simulated follow-up priority,
and clearly illustrative pricing. An authenticated API and working MCP tools let a
future approved assistant retrieve the same information. Basic health checks and
privacy-conscious telemetry support technical review. It performs no treatment,
booking, customer outreach or connection to a company's private systems.

## Example conversations

- A new homeowner: review property characteristics and ask whether an inspection is appropriate.
- An existing customer: combine service history with the property profile to discuss a preventive follow-up.
- A staff assistant: retrieve service options through MCP and explain them for human review.

Potential benefits include more consistent staff preparation, clearer follow-up queues,
and a repeatable way to discuss service options. These benefits have not been measured;
no revenue increase, cost saving or customer outcome is claimed.

## What still needs validation

All records, prices and priority targets are simulated. Tucson and Ohio are independent
demonstration scenarios, not verified company territories. Model scores do not prove
infestation, necessary treatment or timing. Domain experts must review suitability,
priority rules and explanations before any real customer use. The current evaluation
checks a synthetic prediction task, not real-world business effectiveness.

## What an agreed pilot would require

Confirm the intended organization and obtain written authorization. Agree on a narrow
staff-assistance use case, approved service catalog, data access and retention controls.
Use consented, appropriately minimized historical data, define expert-reviewed outcomes,
and retain staff approval of every customer-facing action. Compare the assistant with
existing practice using agreed measures such as staff review time, recommendation
acceptance and inspection outcomes. Set a review point and stop criteria before rollout.

## Proposed next step

A short demonstration and discovery conversation could establish whether there is a
useful pilot problem, the relevant operational constraints, and a suitable employment,
freelance or collaboration arrangement. This is an invitation to discuss potential
work, not a claim of an existing engagement or endorsement.
